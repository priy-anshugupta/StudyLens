Photosynthesis — How Plants Make Food

Plants make their own food through a process called photosynthesis. Chlorophyll, a green pigment in leaves, absorbs light energy from the Sun. Plants take in carbon dioxide from the air through tiny openings called stomata. Roots absorb water from the soil.

During photosynthesis, light energy is used to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose, a sugar that stores chemical energy. Oxygen is released as a by-product. Plants can use glucose for respiration and growth, or store some of it as starch.

Light intensity, the availability of carbon dioxide, and temperature can affect the rate of photosynthesis. A plant kept without light cannot continue photosynthesis, even if water and carbon dioxide are available.
