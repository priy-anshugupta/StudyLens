The Printing Press and the Spread of Ideas

In Europe during the fifteenth century, Johannes Gutenberg developed a printing system using movable metal type. Individual letters could be arranged into a page, inked, and pressed onto paper. The type could then be rearranged and used again.

Printing made it possible to produce many copies of a text more quickly than copying each book by hand. Over time, printed books and pamphlets helped ideas reach wider audiences. The availability of books supported education and the exchange of scientific and religious ideas.

The printing press did not make everyone literate immediately. Access to books still depended on factors such as cost, language, and opportunities for education.
