The Water Cycle

Water moves continuously between the surface of Earth and the atmosphere. Energy from the Sun causes liquid water in oceans, lakes, and rivers to evaporate into water vapor. Plants also release water vapor through transpiration.

As moist air cools, water vapor condenses into tiny liquid droplets that can form clouds. When droplets or ice particles grow large enough, water returns to the surface as precipitation, such as rain or snow.

Some water flows over the land as runoff and enters rivers. Some infiltrates the soil and can become groundwater. Gravity helps move surface water and groundwater toward lower areas. The cycle redistributes water; it does not have one fixed starting point.
